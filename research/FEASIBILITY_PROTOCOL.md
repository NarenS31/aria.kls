# Prospective ARIA feasibility and safety pilot

Status: **draft; requires institutional determination before recruitment**

## Purpose

Determine whether students can use ARIA as intended, whether the logging and
outcome procedures function, and which failures must be repaired before an
efficacy study. This pilot is not powered to establish learning effectiveness.

## Participants

- Proposed population: students in grades 9–12 currently studying content
  represented in the approved task bank.
- Proposed target: approximately 12–20 participants, finalized with the
  reviewing institution.
- Recruitment must avoid teacher or developer pressure. Participation does not
  affect grades, services, or access to ordinary instruction.
- Population-specific recruitment involving ADHD is deferred until the protocol
  is reviewed for appropriate inclusion, language, risks, and subgroup analysis.

## Procedure

1. Obtain approved parental permission and affirmative student assent.
2. Assign a study ID unrelated to school identifiers.
3. Complete a brief baseline content check and confidence rating.
4. Complete two approved tasks with ARIA and one without ARIA.
5. Ask the student to think aloud using neutral instructions.
6. Conduct a short semistructured interview.
7. Have a qualified adult monitor the session and record critical incidents.

## Prespecified feasibility outcomes

- Recruitment and consent rate.
- Completion rate.
- Missing-log rate.
- Median and 95th-percentile response latency.
- Fraction of responses that are empty, irrelevant, factually incorrect,
  answer-revealing, repetitive, or based on invented student actions.
- Student ability to explain what ARIA is asking them to do.
- Requests for human help.
- Student-reported clarity and burden.
- Observed distress, disengagement, or desire to stop.

## Stop and escalation rules

- Students may skip any item or stop immediately without penalty.
- A supervising adult can stop a session for distress, repeated factual errors,
  inappropriate content, privacy exposure, or system instability.
- ARIA must display that it is a research learning tool, not a diagnostic or
  emergency service.
- Unexpected disclosure of imminent harm is handled under the reviewing
  institution's approved safeguarding and mandatory-reporting procedure, not an
  improvised developer response.
- A critical answer-safety or privacy event pauses new enrollment until reviewed.

## Interview prompts

1. What did you think ARIA noticed in your work?
2. Which question helped most, and what did you do because of it?
3. Did any question misunderstand you?
4. Did any response reveal too much or too little?
5. Was there a moment you wanted a person instead?
6. What would make the tool clearer or less distracting?
7. On the final no-ARIA task, did you use anything you practiced earlier?

## Progression criteria

Progression to a controlled study requires:

- all data fields and assignment logs functioning;
- no unresolved critical safety or privacy events;
- prespecified acceptable completion and latency thresholds set before data
  inspection;
- educator review of observed factual and pedagogical failures;
- revision of tasks or policy followed by a second small verification when a
  blocking issue changes the intervention.

All results, including failure rates and non-completion, are reported.
