# Prospective registration: controlled ARIA learning study

Status: **draft, not registered, not institutionally approved**  
Version: 1.0  
Initial confirmatory domain: high-school algebra  
Investigators, school partner, statistician, and registration URL: [pending]

## Research question

Does task-grounded, learner-conditioned ARIA support improve independently
measured algebra learning compared with an active generic-Socratic tutor using
the same task bank, interface, time, and base language model?

## Hypotheses

- **H1, primary:** Students assigned to ARIA score higher on an independently
  authored immediate post-test than students assigned to the active control,
  adjusting for the prespecified pretest.
- **H2, key secondary:** On novel no-tutor tasks, ARIA students show more
  independently human-coded planning, monitoring, justification, checking, and
  self-correction.
- **H3:** ARIA students retain more content on a delayed test 7–14 days later.
- **H4, exploratory:** Effects vary with actual intervention use, baseline
  knowledge, or declared support needs. These analyses are not evidence of an
  ADHD-specific effect.

## Design

- Parallel-group, individually randomized, active-controlled study.
- Allocation 1:1, generated by reproducible code and concealed until enrollment
  and baseline completion.
- Randomization blocked by classroom/site and pretest band.
- Students complete the same approved practice tasks for the same scheduled
  duration.
- `ARIA`: full problem, utterance, learner context, history, and policy gates.
- `Active control`: one generic Socratic question selected without learner
  history or inferred reasoning move.
- Outcome tasks never appear during tutoring.
- Outcome scorers and transcript coders are blinded to condition.

If individual randomization cannot prevent contamination, the design changes to
cluster randomization before registration. Power and analysis must then account
for classroom clustering.

## Participants

- Grades 9–12, currently receiving instruction aligned with the approved task
  domain.
- Inclusion/exclusion criteria are educational and prespecified; disability,
  language background, or low prior achievement are not post-hoc exclusions.
- Parental permission and student assent are obtained unless the reviewing IRB
  approves a different procedure.
- Sample size is set using `eval/power_analysis.py`, pilot variance, expected
  covariate R-squared, attrition, and any classroom ICC. The final assumptions
  and target are frozen before recruitment.

## Outcomes

### Primary

Total score on an independently authored immediate post-test of novel algebra
problems. Two educators validate the scoring key. Constructed responses are
double-scored blind to condition.

### Key secondary

Unprompted strategy-use score on novel problems completed with ARIA absent.
The observable taxonomy is locked before coding. The score is the proportion
of opportunities containing prespecified high-quality planning, justification,
monitoring, evaluation, or self-correction, with correctness reported
separately.

### Additional

- Delayed content-test score at 7–14 days.
- Near-transfer and farther-transfer subscores.
- Confidence–accuracy calibration.
- Completion, time, hint use, human-help requests, and attrition.
- Answer leakage, factual error, invented-action, and safety events.
- Student-reported clarity and perceived ownership, clearly labeled self-report.

## Analysis

- Student is the unit of inference.
- Primary model: post-test score regressed on assignment, centered pretest, and
  prespecified blocking variables; report adjusted mean difference, standardized
  effect, 95% confidence interval, and exact p value.
- Intention-to-treat is primary: analyze students as assigned.
- Cluster-robust or multilevel standard errors are used when design or delivery
  induces classroom/site clustering.
- H1 is the single primary test at two-sided alpha .05.
- H2–H3 form a secondary family controlled with Holm adjustment.
- Treatment-on-treated or dose analyses are exploratory and cannot replace ITT.
- Subgroup interactions are exploratory unless separately powered and
  registered.

## Missing data and attrition

- Enrollment, assignment, exposure, post-test, and delayed-test denominators are
  reported by condition.
- Reasons for withdrawal are recorded when voluntarily provided.
- Primary complete-case and prespecified sensitivity analyses are both reported.
- No participant is excluded for low engagement after randomization.
- Differential attrition and baseline equivalence are assessed following the
  current What Works Clearinghouse standards.

## Fidelity and contamination

- Store task/version IDs, condition, response policy version, latency, failures,
  and intervention exposure.
- Audit a random sample for condition fidelity.
- Ask whether students accessed outside AI or shared prompts.
- Teacher instruction outside the study is documented at the classroom level.

## Claim rule

Only a positive, practically meaningful, uncertainty-bounded H1 result supports
“improved learning in this sample and setting.” Generalization to other
subjects, schools, neurodivergent students, or long-term transfer requires new
evidence. Null and negative findings are published with the same outcomes.
