# ARIA: When Should an AI Math Tutor Stay Quiet?

Most tutoring software replies after every step. ARIA, a student project, asks when an AI math tutor should say nothing. A symbolic math engine checks each step a student types. While the work moves forward, ARIA stays quiet; only after a verified wrong step does a timing model decide whether to offer a hint. That model, a 1995 knowledge-tracing method, was fitted on ASSISTments skill-builder math logs (grade level not stated), not calculus. One honest result: under the 3-correct-in-a-row mastery definition, the quiet setting intervened 52% less often than helping after every wrong step and missed 3.7% of students who went on to get stuck. Stricter definitions shrink that saving. This is a replay of old logs, not a classroom study. Try our "Would you step in?" activity and tell us where we are wrong. We are looking for one teacher willing to talk about a classroom pilot.
